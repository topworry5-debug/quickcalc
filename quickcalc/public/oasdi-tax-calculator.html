What is OASDI Tax?

OASDI tax is the mandatory federal payroll deduction that funds the United States Social Security program. Formally known as Old-Age, Survivors, and Disability Insurance, the tax finances monthly retirement pensions, survivor support for bereaved dependents, and disability benefits for qualifying American workers.

What is the OASDI tax rate?

The employee OASDI tax rate is 6.2% of gross wages, matched by a mandatory 6.2% contribution from the employer for a 12.4% total. Self-employed individuals pay the full 12.4% rate through self-employment tax on 92.35% of net business profits, deducting half the amount on their income tax return.

What is the OASDI wage base limit for 2026?

For the 2026 tax year, the OASDI wage base limit is $184,500. This statutory cap, set by the Social Security Administration based on the national average wage index, means earnings above $184,500 are exempt from Social Security withholding. The maximum annual OASDI tax for any employee in 2026 is $11,439.

Is OASDI the same as Social Security tax?

Yes, OASDI tax and Social Security tax are identical. “OASDI” is the official statutory acronym used by payroll software and the Internal Revenue Service, whereas “Social Security” is the popular name. When the abbreviation appears on a paystub or Form W-2, it denotes standard Social Security withholding.

How OASDI Tax is Calculated: Plain Math & Formulas

Unlike federal income taxes, which use progressive marginal brackets, OASDI is a flat tax with a hard statutory ceiling. The computation formula is:

W-2 Employee OASDI = min(Gross Wages, $184,500 Cap) × 6.2%
Self-Employed OASDI = min(Net SE Earnings × 0.9235, $184,500 Cap) × 12.4%

Real-World Examples for 2026

Example 1: Salary Under Cap ($75,000 / year): Paid biweekly over 26 checks, your gross per check is $2,884.62. Your deduction is $2,884.62 × 6.2% = $178.85 per paycheck. Over the full year, you pay exactly $4,650.00.

Example 2: High Earner Over Cap ($240,000 / year): Paid monthly at $20,000/month, you hit the $184,500 cap in October (Month 10). In October, only $4,500 of your check is taxed ($279.00). In November and December, your OASDI deduction is $0.00, capping your total tax at exactly $11,439.00.

Frequently Asked Questions About OASDI Tax

Why does my paystub say OASDI instead of Social Security?

Most payroll processing providers like ADP, Paychex, and Gusto print the technical legal acronym OASDI on paystubs rather than the colloquial term Social Security. Both refer to the identical mandatory federal program created under the Federal Insurance Contributions Act (FICA).

What does OASDI stand for?

OASDI stands for Old-Age, Survivors, and Disability Insurance. It represents the formal statutory name of the comprehensive federal social insurance initiative established under Title II of the 1935 Social Security Act.

Is OASDI tax mandatory?

Yes, OASDI payroll withholding is compulsory for nearly all United States civilian employees and self-employed workers under federal tax law. Limited exemptions apply only to specific state and local government personnel covered by alternative qualifying pension programs, certain religious sects, and nonresident foreign students on specific exchange visas.

Do self-employed people pay OASDI?

Yes, self-employed freelancers, independent contractors, and business owners pay OASDI through the Self-Employment Contributions Act (SECA). Because they have no employer covering the other half, self-employed workers pay the full 12.4% rate, assessed on 92.35% of their net business profits up to the annual wage cap.

What happens when I hit the OASDI wage base limit?

Once your cumulative gross earnings for the calendar year cross the annual wage base limit ($184,500 in 2026), your employer immediately ceases withholding the 6.2% OASDI tax. Your take-home paycheck will noticeably increase for the remainder of that year, resetting back to the normal 6.2% deduction on January 1.

Is OASDI tax deductible on my income tax return?

W-2 employees cannot deduct their 6.2% OASDI payroll taxes on federal Form 1040. In contrast, self-employed individuals can claim an above-the-line deduction for exactly 50% of their total self-employment tax (representing the employer-equivalent share) on Schedule 1 of Form 1040, which lowers their adjusted gross income.

OASDI vs Medicare tax — what is the difference?

While both taxes make up FICA payroll deductions, OASDI taxes are capped at $184,500 for 2026 at a 6.2% employee rate to fund retirement, survivor, and disability pensions. Medicare tax is 1.45% for employees, has no wage cap whatsoever, and taxes every dollar of earnings to fund hospital insurance, with an extra 0.9% surtax on high earners.

What is the OASDI tax rate for 2026?

For the 2026 tax year, the employee OASDI tax rate remains 6.2% on covered wages up to $184,500, with an identical 6.2% paid by employers. Self-employed individuals pay the combined 12.4% rate up to the same $184,500 threshold.

Does everyone pay the same OASDI rate?

Yes, all covered American workers pay the flat statutory 6.2% rate regardless of tax bracket, up until they hit the annual wage base cap. Because higher earners stop paying once they exceed $184,500 in 2026, OASDI is classified as a regressive payroll tax on total income.

Can I get OASDI tax back if I overpaid?

If you worked for a single employer, excess withholding rarely happens because payroll systems automatically stop at the cap. However, if you switched employers mid-year or held multiple concurrent jobs earning over $184,500 collectively, each company withheld up to the cap. You can reclaim the excess overpaid OASDI as a refundable tax credit on Line 11 of IRS Form 1040 Schedule 3.
